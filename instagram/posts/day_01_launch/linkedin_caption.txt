I'm starting a daily writing habit: plain-language cybersecurity, AI, and privacy — one real fact, one real fix, every day.

Here's why. Most security advice I come across falls into one of two buckets: it's too technical to actually act on, or it's vague enough to be useless ("just be careful online"). Neither one helps the people I actually know — and in most workplaces, "the people I actually know" are exactly who a phishing email or a reused password ends up costing the most.

So I'm building the middle ground. Daily posts on current threats and everyday safety habits. Weekly breakdowns of real scams and what AI is actually changing about how they work. And a small set of free tools — a password coach, a breach checker, a 2FA recovery kit builder — that teach the underlying habit instead of just handing over a one-time answer.

Why this matters for your work life specifically: the same credential-stuffing attack that empties someone's personal email is the one that gets used against a work account with a reused password. The same AI voice-cloning scam that targets a "grandkid in trouble" call works just as well as a "wire transfer, urgent" call from a spoofed executive. The habits are the same. The stakes are just higher when it's not just your data on the line.

Not a company, not a bot — just one person writing this properly, one post at a time.

Tomorrow's post: the password mistake nobody thinks is a big deal. (It is — and it's probably in your password manager right now.)
