"Your package couldn't be delivered. Click here to fix it." 📦

That text is called smishing — SMS phishing. The wording changes every time, but the pattern underneath almost never does: a name you trust (your bank, a delivery company), a manufactured problem that needs fixing right now, and a link that promises to fix it.

It works because a text feels more urgent and more "official" than an email ever does, and almost nobody's ever been taught to question one.

The fix: don't tap the link. Open the company's official app instead, or type their website in yourself. If something's genuinely wrong, it'll be sitting right there too — no scam link required.
