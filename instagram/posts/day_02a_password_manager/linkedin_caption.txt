The password mistake nobody thinks is a big deal: reusing the same one across multiple accounts. I'd bet most of us have done it at some point — it's just easier to remember one password than fifty.

Here's why it's worth five minutes to fix. When a random site gets breached — and eventually, one of the sites you've signed up for will be — that leaked email/password combination gets automatically tried against hundreds of other services. It's called credential stuffing, and it's fully automated. If you reused that password anywhere else, including a work account, that account is now exposed too, with zero effort from the attacker.

The fix is simpler than people expect. Your phone almost certainly already has a free password manager built in (iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager) that can run a checkup showing exactly which passwords are reused or already compromised, then generate a unique one for each account going forward.

Why this matters for your work life specifically: IT security teams spend a lot of effort on perimeter defense, but a reused personal password is a door that bypasses all of it. Fixing your own password hygiene is one of the few security improvements an individual can make that has zero dependency on your employer's tooling.

Start with email, banking, and payment-linked accounts first — the rest can catch up over time.
